How to play

You, Abhimanyu

Gold ring. Each turn you move one tile, wait or scout.

Gates

Gaps in a wall, marked by torches and a banner. Walls can only be crossed at gates.

Faded walls and gates

Ones you saw earlier. The formation turns, so they may have moved since.

Misty walls

Walls you have never seen. Anything could be there.

Allies (blue)

An ally next to you blocks sentinel pressure. An ally next to a sentinel pins it in place.

Sentinels (red)

If one ends a turn next to you, you lose 1 resolve. You can force your way past one for 2 resolve.

Jayadratha (purple)

Once you break in, he seals the outer gates. A gate with an ally standing on it stays open.

Drona

Every 5 turns his drums sound and the walls turn, so the gates move.

Goal: reach the golden lotus at the centre, then get back out through an outer gate that is still open. Ghost columns mark where you last saw an enemy.

Paused

Your thinking time is not counted while the game is paused.

About this project

Kurukshetra is a Mini Research & Innovation Project, B.Tech II Year, Amrita Vishwa Vidyapeetham, Chennai. Theme: Strategic Lessons from the Mahabharata.

  1. Mahabharata. Abhimanyu breaks into the Chakravyuha. He knows only half of what he needs, his allies are held back at the breach, and the formation keeps turning.
  2. Strategic insight. Decisions made with partial, changing information, by people who each know something different.
  3. Contemporary problem. Teams act on incomplete or stale information, and coordination failures turn good individual choices into bad outcomes.
  4. The game. A human and autonomous agents each hold a different slice of the truth. The amount of information and the level of coordination are experimental conditions.
  5. Evidence. Every move is logged against what you believed and what was true, so information errors and decision errors can be told apart.

Sessions are stored only in this browser. Export them from the Analysis dashboard.
